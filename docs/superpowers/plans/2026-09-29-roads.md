# Roads Plan

**Goal:** People can build roads, and later upgrade them from rough tracks to paved roads. A road makes a tile quicker to cross for every traveller, and lets a civilization's influence reach further along it. This is slice 4 of `2026-09-29-territory.md`.

## What exists today

- **Travel.** `travel.entry_cost(world_map, tile)` gives every tile a fixed cost: grassland 10, forest/desert/tundra 15, mountain 30, water impassable. Journeys, envoys, expeditions and the influence field all use it.
- **Construction.** `start_project` builds a shelter or storage at the capital. The work happens at home, needs no travel, and ends in `building_completed`.
- **Internal journeys.** `found_settlement`, `station_garrison` and `relocate_group` already move committed people along explicit routes, with provisions, hazards and permanent death, and walk them home when an arrival check fails.

## Research summary

- **Why roads matter.** Across pre-modern states, the reach of power followed roads: the Persian Royal Road, Roman *viae*, and the Inca *Qhapaq Ñan*. Michael Mann's "infrastructural power" is literally the ability to move people and orders cheaply.
- **Speed.** A good road roughly doubled a walker's daily distance on open ground: a Roman legion managed about 30 km a day on a road, and much less cross-country. In steep country the gain is smaller in absolute terms, but still large. Stanford ORBIS models road travel at about 1.5–2× the speed of rough going.
- **Cost.** Roads were very labour-intensive: a surfaced Roman road took hundreds of person-days per kilometre. We need a game-scale figure, roughly *a few crew-weeks per tile*, so a road is a real investment but not an impossible one.
- **Roads serve everyone.** A road is a physical thing, and an enemy marches on it as easily as its builder. Both *Europa Universalis V* and *Civilization* apply roads to any unit that uses the tile. Hiding a road from enemies is a war-slice concern (destroying it), not a rule of travel.
- **Game precedents.**
  - *Europa Universalis V*: roads lower the cost of the path that control decays along.
  - *Old World* and *Civilization*: workers build roads tile by tile, so a partly built road is already useful.
  - *Hearts of Iron IV*: supply follows infrastructure.

## Proposed design

### 1. Road tiles and grades

- A new `Road` record holds:
  - the tile;
  - its **grade**;
  - the civilization that last worked on it;
  - the day it was first built, and the day it last changed grade.
- `WorldState.roads` is a sorted tuple with at most one road per tile.
- Roads cannot be built on water. Bridges and fords are out of scope.
- Roads never decay in this slice. Wear and destruction belong with war.

A road evolves through six grades:

| Grade | What it is | Materials per tile | Crew needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. **Footpath** | Trodden path, undergrowth cut back | none | nothing |
| 2. **Track** | Cleared cart-width track | none | nothing |
| 3. **Graded road** | Levelled and cambered, with ditches and timber culverts | 3 timber | nothing |
| 4. **Gravel road** | Graded road with a packed gravel bed | 5 stone | nothing |
| 5. **Paved road** | Laid stone surface with kerbs | 15 stone | at least one living stoneworking practitioner |
| 6. **Highway** | Wide paved road with stone bridges over streams and waymarked stages | 20 stone, 2 tools | at least one living stoneworking practitioner |

- **Upgrades go one grade at a time.** A tile passes through every grade in order. A crew ordered to a higher grade does each missing step in turn, and every step it finishes counts at once.
- **Materials tie roads to the economy.**
  - Footpaths and tracks cost only labour, so any civilization can start.
  - A graded road needs timber.
  - A gravel road needs stone.
  - A highway also consumes tools, which the civilization must first craft.
- **Why the top two grades need stoneworking.** Stoneworking is one of the regional starting capabilities. Only civilizations that start with it can pave until the skill spreads, through teaching or through migrants who carry it. Paved roads and highways therefore become a regional advantage that can travel. Knowledge stays in people: if the crew's last stoneworker dies on the road, work above gravel stops, and the crew finishes at the grade it has reached.
- **Grades left unused.** Surveying would suit the upper grades, but no civilization can currently gain it, so nothing depends on it. A later knowledge slice could make highways need it.

### 2. What a road does

**Travel cost.** Entry costs, in tenths of a day:

| Terrain | None | Footpath | Track | Graded | Gravel | Paved | Highway |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Grassland | 10 | 9 | 8 | 7 | 6 | 5 | 4 |
| Forest, desert, tundra | 15 | 13 | 11 | 9 | 8 | 6 | 5 |
| Mountain | 30 | 26 | 22 | 18 | 15 | 12 | 10 |

The territory plan suggested a flat cost of 6 for every road tile. These costs depend on terrain instead, because a highway over a mountain pass should still be slower than a meadow footpath. In rough country the early grades give the biggest gains, which matches history: the first cleared path mattered most there. On open grassland each grade gives about the same small gain.

- `entry_cost` takes the road grade of each tile, and every traveller uses it: journeys, internal journeys, envoys and expeditions, of any civilization.
- **Influence.** The influence field uses the same costs. A 32-person capital projects 40 influence about 4 grassland tiles out. Along a road it reaches about: footpath 4, track 5, graded 5, gravel 6, paved 8 and highway 10 tiles. Territory therefore grows along roads, as the spec intends, without any painted border.
- **Supply.** Supply connectivity already depends only on whether a path exists, not on its cost, so it is unchanged. War will later let roads matter to supply by being cut.
- **Timing.** Travel costs are read on the day a tile is entered. Provisions are packed at dispatch using the costs of that day, so a road finished mid-journey only means food is left over.

### 3. Building and upgrading

**New order `build_road`.** It carries a target grade, from footpath to highway. It is an internal journey of a new kind, `ROADWORK`. A crew of 1–16 people sets out from one of the civilization's own settlements along an explicit route. They raise each tile along the route to the target grade, then walk home.

- **Labour.** Each step needs a multiple of the tile's base entry cost, in person-days:

  | Step | Multiple | Grassland | Forest, desert, tundra | Mountain |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | to footpath | 1× | 10 | 15 | 30 |
  | to track | 2× | 20 | 30 | 60 |
  | to graded | 3× | 30 | 45 | 90 |
  | to gravel | 4× | 40 | 60 | 120 |
  | to paved | 6× | 60 | 90 | 180 |
  | to highway | 8× | 80 | 120 | 240 |

  An 8-person crew cuts a grassland footpath in 2 days, and takes it to a track in 3 more. Taking the tile from nothing all the way to highway is 240 person-days, which is 30 days for that crew. A mountain highway takes 90 days.
- **Materials.** Timber, stone and tools for every step on the route are taken from the common store at dispatch, the same way provisions are. Materials for steps that are never built go back into the store when the crew returns.
- **Tiles already at or above the target grade**, whoever built them, are walked over and not worked. Upgrading another civilization's road on your own land is allowed, and the record then names you as the last to work on it.
- **Provisions.** Packed for the walk out, the work days and the walk home, then used under the same foraging and hunger rules as other journeys. A crew that runs out of food stops work and turns home.
- **Hazards.** The daily hazard and delay rolls apply on work days as well as travel days. Death is permanent, and a crew whose members have all died leaves its unfinished tiles as they were.
- **Where roads may go.** Validation checks only what the civilization knows:
  - the route must be over known land;
  - it must not enter a tile the civilization has seen owned by another civilization;
  - a `paved` or `highway` order needs a stoneworking practitioner in the crew;
  - the store must hold the materials for every step.

  When the crew reaches each tile, the engine checks the real world. If another civilization now owns the tile, work stops there, the crew turns home, and the sender receives a private notice.
- **Committed people.** Crew members are away. They do no work or teaching at home, are not residents, cannot take other orders, and a mother with a birth due cannot join.

### 4. Private knowledge

- A civilization knows every road it built.
- It learns of other roads when one of its people sees the tile.
- It learns a road's grade by observation as well: `Observation.observed_road` holds the grade seen, or nothing.
- Council reports add `known_roads`: its own roads, plus roads it has observed, each with its grade and observation day. A rival's later upgrade stays unknown until someone sees it.
- Validation never needs to know about roads, because the engine computes travel time from the real world. A sovereign uses `known_roads` to plan routes, and its people find out what an unseen road is worth only by travelling on it.

### 5. Events

- `road_crew_dispatched`;
- `road_built`, one for each step on each tile, with the new grade;
- `road_work_stopped`, when a tile is foreign-owned on arrival, the crew runs out of food, or its last stoneworker dies before the stone-laying grades;
- `road_crew_returned`.

## Out of scope

- Road decay and maintenance.
- Destroying or blocking roads. This is part of war.
- Bridges and rivers.
- Tolls, and treaty rights to use roads.
- Per-settlement supply depots.

## Validation

- **Focused tests:**
  - road costs for each terrain and grade;
  - a crew builds a road tile by tile, and a half-built road already speeds travel;
  - upgrades go one grade at a time, and a crew ordered to highway does every missing step;
  - each grade consumes its own materials, and unused materials come back;
  - a tile already at or above the target grade is walked over, not rebuilt;
  - paved and highway grades need a stoneworker in the crew, and stop if the last one dies;
  - another civilization's journey benefits from a road it did not build;
  - influence reaches further along a road, and territory follows over time;
  - a tile found foreign-owned on arrival stops the work and sends the crew home;
  - a crew that runs out of food stops work;
  - a crew that dies en route leaves the rest of the road unbuilt;
  - unused stone returns on arrival home;
  - validation rejects water, unknown tiles and known foreign land;
  - observed roads appear in reports and unseen ones do not;
  - replay and state hashes are unchanged across reruns.
- **Acceptance test:** a footpath from a capital to a colony is later upgraded to a gravel road. Each grade shortens later journeys, and territory grows along the road.
- **Stress tests:** in the territory matrix, the expanding sovereigns also build a footpath to their colony and then upgrade it, under the same daily invariants as before (no flicker, anchored settlements, deterministic replay).

## As built

- **Several tiles a day.** Before roads, a party entered at most one tile a day, so a road below 10 tenths of a day would have gained nothing on grassland. Worse, unspent walking would have piled up without limit. Journeys, envoys and explorers now enter as many tiles as a day's walking covers. Unroaded travel is unchanged, because every roadless tile costs at least a full day.
  - A party that halts, whether it has arrived, turned back, or stopped to work, keeps less than one day of unspent walking.
  - Explorers observe every tile they enter, including its road grade.
- **The crew's first tile is its own settlement.** The crew raises every tile on its route, starting with the settlement it leaves from, so the road runs right into town.
- **Materials are hauled separately.** Timber, stone and tools are taken from the store at dispatch but do not count against the 50 units each person can carry. Otherwise any paved road would be impossible.
- **Crews turn home before they go hungry.** A crew packs what it can carry, up to 50 days of food each. It stops work and turns home once its pack holds only enough for the walk back plus the usual margin. This replaces "stops when out of food", and it means a long job is done over several trips.
- **Stop reasons.** A crew that turns back early ends with the new outcome `stopped`, and a `road_work_stopped` event gives one of these reasons:
  - `foreign_land`;
  - `provisions`;
  - `no_stoneworker`;
  - `materials`, a safeguard that should never fire, because roads never lose grade.
- **Planning uses known roads only.** Provisions and materials are both worked out from the roads the civilization knows of, so a validation error never reveals an unseen road.
- **Road knowledge.** When a crew finishes a grade, the civilization records the tile as observed, at the new grade, that day.
- **Stress runs.** The expanding sovereigns send an 8-person crew to raise a track to their first colony at each later council, so every tile passes through footpath and then track. Each day the stress run also checks three things:
  - no road lies on water;
  - a road never loses grade;
  - a road never disappears.
