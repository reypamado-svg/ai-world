# War, Occupation and Surrender Plan

**Goal:** Civilizations can fight. War is carried out by real people who march, eat, fight and die. Its consequences are permanent: deaths, wounds, prisoners, lost goods, occupied land and, in the end, surrender and changes of allegiance. This is slice 5 of `2026-09-29-territory.md`. It covers the conflict and endgame parts of the civilization-layer design (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-27-civilization-layer-design.md`).

The user's decisions are recorded in the Decisions section and applied throughout. W1 is split into three builds: W1a, W1b and W1c.

## What the design spec requires

- **Combat factors.** Combat outcomes depend on numbers, training, equipment, terrain, fortification, preparation, leadership, morale, weather, intelligence and supply.
- **Consequences.** Conflict produces permanent deaths, injuries, prisoners, displacement, destroyed assets, resource loss, disease risk, and delayed or incorrect reports.
- **Campaigns** are multi-day projects with explicit people, equipment, supply requirements, objectives and routes. A command never creates a combat outcome by itself.
- **Loyalty and surrender.**
  - Citizens stay loyal while their civilization functions, and occupation alone never transfers allegiance.
  - A sovereign may surrender, release its people, and direct them to join another civilization.
  - A society with no functioning settlements may dissolve gradually.
- **Endings.** A civilization is eliminated only when no living member remains. One-survivor and zero-survivor endings are recorded exactly.
- **No forced conflict.** The engine never forces a final war; a long peace is a valid outcome.

## What exists today

| Building block | State | Use in war |
|---|---|---|
| People | Sex, age, health (basis points), hunger debt, disease load, skills, location, and permanent death | Fighters, casualties, wounds |
| Journeys | Explicit routes, terrain costs, roads, provisions, foraging, hazards, and turning back | Campaigns are journeys that fight |
| Garrisons and settlements | Defenders standing on a tile | Targets and defenders |
| Territory | Derived control with challenges, supply cut-off already defined (halved influence), and `route_severed` events | Occupation and cut supply lines |
| Goods | Stores, toll chests, cargo in transit, and axes, tools and metal in stores | Plunder and equipment |
| Treaties | Trade and migration terms, cancellation and repudiation; the `peace` kind exists but does nothing | War breaks treaties, and peace ends war |
| Missing | Weather, fortifications, a fighting skill, prisoners, and a record of allegiance history | Added below or deferred |

## Research summary

- **Pre-state and early warfare was mostly raids.**
  - Keeley (*War Before Civilization*, 1996) and Gat (*War in Human Civilization*, 2006) find that ambushes and raids, not pitched battles, did most of the killing among small societies.
  - Our civilizations start with 32 founders, so the first wars will look like raids between villages, not clashes of armies.
- **Numbers help, though not as the square.** Lanchester (1916) showed that aimed fire makes strength grow with the square of numbers. Hand-to-hand fighting is closer to his *linear* law, where each fighter engages only a few enemies at a time. Numbers matter, but not quadratically.
- **Most deaths come in the rout.**
  - Krentz ("Casualties in Hoplite Battles", 1985) estimates victors lost about 5% and the defeated about 14%.
  - Ancient accounts agree that most killing happened after a line broke and fled.
  - Battles were won by **breaking morale**, not by killing everyone.
- **The defender's edge.** Rough terrain, walls and preparation favour defenders. The "3:1" attacker rule of thumb is disputed, but the direction is not.
- **Supply limits campaigns.**
  - Engels (*Alexander the Great and the Logistics of the Macedonian Army*, 1978) puts a soldier's need at about 1.4 kg of grain a day.
  - An army could carry only a couple of weeks' food, and foraging stripped the land it passed through.
  - Our provisions and foraging model already captures this.
- **Sieges.** Most pre-modern sieges ended through hunger, disease, betrayal or negotiated surrender rather than storming, and surrender "on terms" was the norm.
- **Occupation is not ownership.** Under the Hague Regulations (1907, Article 43), an occupier administers territory but does not acquire sovereignty. *Europa Universalis IV* likewise keeps the owner and the occupying controller separate. Our territory layer already separates claims from control, and occupation becomes a third layer.
- **Game precedents.**
  - *Crusader Kings III:* levies, supply limits, and attrition in hostile land.
  - *Dwarf Fortress:* individual wounds and deaths.
  - *Battle Brothers* and *Total War:* morale breaks decide fights.
  - *Europa Universalis IV:* occupation and negotiated peace.
  - The shared lesson: resolve fights through **morale, terrain and supply**, and record every casualty by name.

## Proposed slicing

War is too large for one change. Recommended order:

1. **W1: armies and battle.** Built in three steps:
   - **W1a: hostility, war parties and combat.** Declared wars and undeclared raids, war parties of any size, battles that kill, wound and rout real people, plunder, drilling, and battle experience.
   - **W1b: equipment, unit types and siege engines.** An armoury order to craft weapons. Unit types follow from each fighter's kit, and the kits a civilization can make depend on its knowledge. Ladders, rams and catapults.
   - **W1c: military research.** A research order through which scholars discover new military knowledge that unlocks better units, engines and doctrines.
2. **W2: occupation and sieges.** Holding enemy land and settlements, cutting supply, sieges that starve rather than storm, and destroying roads and seizing chests.
3. **W3: prisoners, surrender and allegiance.** Captives, surrender terms, allegiance transfer that keeps personal history, and peace treaties that end wars.
4. **W4: dissolution, elimination and the end of the world.** A civilization with no functioning settlements dissolves, one with no living members is eliminated, and one-survivor and zero-survivor endings are detected.

## W1a in detail

### 1. Hostility

- **New order `declare_war`.** It is sent to a known civilization as a diplomatic message carried by an envoy, like a treaty cancellation.
- **Breaking treaties.** On dispatch, the declarer is at war and every treaty between the two is repudiated. That treaty breach already has rules and events. A civilization cannot declare war on another and stay bound to it by treaty.
- **When the target learns.** The target learns it is at war when the declaration is delivered, or earlier if it is attacked. A surprise attack before delivery is possible and recorded as such.
- **War state.** Each war is recorded with the day it started, the declarer, and each side's first knowledge of it. War between the two continues until a peace treaty (W3) or until one side is eliminated (W4).
- **Undeclared raids are allowed.** A war party may attack a civilization it is not at war with.
  - The attack itself starts a war, recorded as *undeclared*, and breaks every treaty between the two at that moment, counted as a breach by the attacker.
  - The victim learns of the war at once, because it is being attacked.
  - A later reputation or diplomatic-memory system can make undeclared attacks costly. For now the record, and the broken treaties, are the consequence.

### 2. War parties

- **New order `send_war_party`.** It is a journey of a new kind, `campaign`. Fighters from one of the civilization's settlements march an explicit route over known land, with provisions and equipment taken from the store.
- **No fixed size limit.** A civilization can send as many able fighters as it has, from one settlement. A war party is limited only by:
  - its people;
  - the food each fighter can carry, 50 units;
  - the equipment in store.

  Other journeys keep their limit of 16.
- **Mixing.** A war party may mix unit types (W1b) and carry siege engines (W1b).
- **Equipment.** Axes, and later metal weapons, are carried one per fighter and return home with survivors.
- **Objectives**, set at the end of the route:
  - **raid:** attack whoever defends the target tile. If the fight is won, carry off goods from the store or chest there, up to what the party can bear, and march home. Plunder is taken only when fighters physically stand in the settlement.
  - **attack:** fight the defenders of a garrison or settlement, then hold the tile. Holding it becomes occupation in W2; in W1 the party then marches home.
- **Encounters on the road.** A war party that enters a tile holding hostile people fights them. This covers garrisons, residents, and other war parties. Hostile shipments and migrants on the road are ambushed: their goods are seized and they flee home. Capturing them waits for W3.
- **Everything else is as for other journeys.** War parties travel on terrain and roads, pay no tolls to enemies, and suffer delays, hazards and hunger.

### 3. Combat

**Fighting strength**, in integer basis points, for each fighter is 100 multiplied by these factors, all rounded deterministically:

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Health | health_bp / 10 000 |
| Age | full from 16 to 50 years; 60% from 13 to 15 and from 51 to 60; otherwise not a fighter |
| Hunger | −1% for each point of nutrition debt, down to −50% |
| Arms | +50% with an axe, +80% with a metal weapon (later) |
| Arms skill | +1% for each point of the new `arms` skill, up to +60%; see "Drill and battle experience" |
| Unit type | from the fighter's kit; see W1b |

**Defender bonuses:**

- ×1.25 on forest;
- ×1.5 on mountain;
- ×1.25 inside a settlement.

Fortifications arrive with a later building slice; the multiplier is defined now so they can plug in.

**A battle is fought in daily rounds** on one tile.

- **Losses.** Each round, each side suffers losses that grow with the other side's strength relative to its own. The rate is calibrated so that an even fight costs each side about 3–5% a day.
- **Wounds and deaths.**
  - A loss is a **wound**: health falls by 3 000–6 000.
  - A wounded fighter whose health would drop to 0 dies. Deaths are permanent.
  - Every person-level outcome is drawn from a named stream, `day:{d}:war:{battle}:…`, so replays match exactly.
- **Morale and rout.** A side **breaks** when its losses in the battle pass its morale threshold:
  - 30% for a war party;
  - 50% for defenders of their own settlement.

  Hunger and being cut off from supply lower the threshold by up to half.
- **After a break:**
  - The routed side flees. Attackers flee home; defenders flee to their nearest own settlement, or scatter and hide if none is reachable.
  - Pursuit costs the routed side a further 10% of its strength that day, which reproduces the historical pattern that most deaths come in the rout.
- **The winner** keeps the tile and gains `arms` experience.
- **Non-combatants.**
  - Children, the elderly and the sick in a settlement never fight.
  - A settlement whose defenders break is **taken**. Raiders plunder it. Occupation, and what happens to its people, come in W2 and W3.

### 4. Drill and battle experience

A fighter's `arms` skill comes from two sources, and battle teaches more, faster and further than drill.

- **Drill.**
  - **Order:** the new `drill` order assigns people to drill for a number of days.
  - **Gain:** each drilling person gains 1 `arms` point every 5 days of drill.
  - **Cap:** drill alone takes a fighter to at most 20.
  - **Cost:** drilling people do no other work those days, so drilling costs output.
- **Battle.**
  - **Survival:** each battle a fighter survives gives 3 points, 5 on the winning side.
  - **Cap:** battle experience can take a fighter up to 60.
  - **Veterans:** a fighter with 30 or more, which only battle can reach, is a **veteran**. For every 10% of a side who are veterans, that side's morale threshold rises by 2 points, so veterans hold the line longer.
  - **Resilience:** veterans also take 10% less from wounds.
- **Why battle is worth it:** one battle gives as much experience as 15 to 25 days of drill, the ceiling rises from 20 to 60, and only battle makes veterans.

### 5. Knowledge and reports

- **Each side learns only what its own people witnessed.** That covers the enemy it saw, its own dead and wounded by name, and the other side's losses as an estimate rounded to the nearest 5 people. The estimate is exact only for enemy dead left on the field.
- **Reports from a war party reach home only when survivors return.** A party that dies to the last person is known only as missing. This is the "delayed or incorrect reports" rule.
- **Reports gain:**
  - `wars`;
  - `war_reports`, per battle as known;
  - known enemy parties, as sighted.

### 6. Events

- `war_declared`, `war_learned`, `undeclared_attack`;
- `drill_started`, `drill_completed`;
- `war_party_dispatched`, `battle_joined`, `battle_round`, `side_broke`, `battle_won`;
- `person_wounded`, and `person_died` with the cause `battle`;
- `settlement_raided`, `goods_plundered`, `convoy_ambushed`;
- `war_party_returned`.

## W1b: equipment, unit types and siege engines

### Armoury

- **New order `craft_equipment`.** It names a kit, a number, and the workers who make it at home.
- **Requirements.** Each kit has materials, a labour cost in person-days, and a required capability. At least one worker must practise that capability. Knowledge lives in people, as it does elsewhere.
- **Output.** Finished kits go into the store as new resources: `spear`, `sling`, `bow`, `bronze_arms` and so on. Axes already exist.

### Unit types

A fighter's type is decided by the kit it carries from the store when the war party sets out.

| Unit | Kit, made from | Needs | Strength | Special |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Levy | none | — | ×1.0 | — |
| Slinger | sling (1 timber) | — | ×1.1 | ranged: strikes in a volley before melee, weak in melee |
| Spearman | spear (2 timber) | timbercraft | ×1.4 | +25% when defending |
| Axeman | axe (existing) | — | ×1.5 | +10% when attacking |
| Archer | bow (3 timber) | archery (W1c) | ×1.2 | stronger volley than slingers, weak in melee |
| Heavy infantry | bronze arms (3 metal) | bronze working (W1c) | ×1.8 | morale threshold +5 points |

- **Volleys.** Each battle's first round opens with a volley from the ranged fighters of both sides before melee.
- **Knowledge is uneven.** Which kits a civilization can make depends on the capabilities its people hold. Only civilizations that start with, learn, or research the right knowledge field better troops. Knowledge spreads by teaching and migration, and can also be researched (W1c).

### Siege engines

Engines are built like kits, and carried and crewed by the war party.

| Engine | Made from | Needs | Crew | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ladders | 4 timber | — | 2 | halves the defenders' settlement bonus |
| Ram | 10 timber, 4 planks | timbercraft | 4 | removes the settlement bonus; breaches walls in W2 |
| Catapult | 15 timber, 10 stone | siegecraft (W1c) | 6 | bombards before each round, and damages walls in W2 |

- **Crew.** The crew fight too, but at half strength while crewing.
- **Weight.** Engines slow the whole party: rams and catapults add half again to every tile's travel cost.
- **Capture and loss.** A routed party abandons its engines, and the winner may take them.

## W1c: military research

- **New order `research`.** It assigns scholars at home to a topic. Each scholar adds 1 research point a day, and 5 points more for each scholar who practises writing. Scholars do no other work.
- **Discovery.** When a topic's cost is met, the civilization discovers it as a capability. The scholars are its first practitioners, and it then spreads like any other knowledge.
- **Topics.**

| Topic | Cost (points) | Needs | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Archery | 300 | timbercraft | bows and archers |
| Spear formations | 200 | — | spearmen get +10% more when defending |
| Drill doctrine | 250 | — | drill gains twice as fast, and the drill cap rises from 20 to 30 |
| Bronze working | 400 | metallurgy awareness | bronze arms and heavy infantry |
| Siegecraft | 400 | timbercraft, stoneworking | catapults |
| Military logistics | 300 | — | fighters carry 60 units of food instead of 50 |
| Fortification | 400 | stoneworking | walls (W2) |

- **Loss.** A civilization that loses every practitioner of a researched capability loses it, as with other knowledge. Research can be repeated.

## Decisions

1. **How war starts.** Formal declarations *and* undeclared raids are both allowed. An undeclared raid starts the war by itself and breaks every treaty at that moment.
2. **War party size.** There is no fixed limit. A civilization can field as large an army as its people, food and equipment allow.
3. **Fighting skill.** It comes from both drill and battle. Battle is the stronger incentive: faster gains, a higher ceiling, and veteran morale and resilience that drill alone cannot reach.
4. **Order.** W1 comes first, as W1a, then W1b, then W1c. Each goes in its own pull request, separate from roads and tolls.
5. **Additions from the user.** Unit types by technology level (W1b), siege engines (W1b), and military research (W1c).

## Validation (W1a, when built)

- **Focused tests:**
  - war needs a declaration on record, and declaring breaks every treaty;
  - the target learns of the war on delivery or on attack;
  - fighting strength follows health, age, hunger, arms and experience;
  - undeclared raids start a war and break treaties;
  - war parties larger than 16 march and fight;
  - drill and battle raise `arms` to their caps, and veterans hold longer;
  - terrain and settlement bonuses help defenders;
  - daily losses, wounds and deaths are permanent and named;
  - a side breaks at its morale threshold, and the rout costs more;
  - raiders plunder only on a win, and only what they can carry;
  - ambushed convoys lose their goods;
  - each side's report holds only what it witnessed, and arrives late or never.
- **Acceptance test:** two neighbours go from trade to war. A raid is repulsed, a second raid succeeds and carries off food, casualties are recorded by name on both sides, and replays and hashes match.
- **Stress tests:** war matrices across seeds check every day that:
  - no one fights who is not at war;
  - the dead never fight or return;
  - goods are conserved through plunder;
  - no report holds knowledge its civilization never witnessed.

W1b and W1c each get their own focused and acceptance tests when they are built:

- crafting needs practitioners and materials;
- unit bonuses and volleys;
- engines negate settlement bonuses;
- research accumulates, needs prerequisites, and yields capabilities that spread and can be lost.

## W1a as built

- **Battles last a day.**
  - A battle is fought in rounds on the day the war party meets the enemy, since pre-modern battles rarely lasted longer.
  - It has at most 20 rounds. A battle still undecided after 20 rounds counts as the attackers giving up the field.
  - Battles between the same two sides on later days are new battles.
- **Calibration.**
  - An even fight costs each side 5% of its fighters a round.
  - A war party breaks at 20% losses, and defenders of their own settlement at 30%.
  - A wound in battle takes 3 000–12 000 health, and a wound in the rout 6 000–15 000.
  - With these numbers, winners lose about 3–5% dead and losers about 10–12%, close to Krentz's figures.
  - The plan's 30% and 50% thresholds made small raids last many rounds and drift into stalemate.
- **Undeclared attacks.** A war starts at the first hostile act. That is a battle, an ambush, or a war party reaching its target, whichever comes first. The victim learns of it on the spot.
- **Who fights.** Defenders are every able person of the enemy on the tile, except those on convoys, migrations and other non-fighting journeys.
  - Settlement and garrison defenders arm themselves with axes from their civilization's store.
  - Defending war parties use the axes they carry.
- **Broken home defenders stay put.** They hide rather than flee, and the raiders plunder the settlement. Driving people out waits for occupation (W2) and captivity (W3).
- **Plunder** comes from the target civilization's common store when raiders stand in any of its settlements, and from a toll chest on that tile, in a fixed order starting with food.
  - Raiders carry only what fits in the room their eaten food has freed.
  - Plunder reaches the raiders' store when they return, and is lost if they perish.
- **Ambushes** happen when a war party ends its day on the same tile as an enemy convoy or other party. The convoy loses what the raiders can carry and flees home as `ambushed`.
  - Parties that pass each other in the middle of a day do not meet. This is a known simplification.
- **At the target.** A war party at its target turns home the same day. `attack` fights, then marches home; holding the tile comes with W2.
- **Supply.** Supply does not yet affect morale; every side counts as supplied until W2 cuts supply lines.
- **Reports.**
  - Home defenders report at once.
  - A war party's battles are reported when its survivors return, and never if it perishes.
  - The winner counts the enemy dead exactly. Everything else about the enemy is rounded to the nearest 5.
  - Sighting reports of enemy parties were not built.
- **Drill** is recorded per group with a length of 1–180 days. Drilling people eat but neither farm nor work.
- **Stress runs.** The war matrix runs 12 seeds, with declared wars on even seeds and undeclared raids on odd ones. Each day it checks that:
  - battles happen only within a war;
  - the dead never fight or return;
  - stone and timber are conserved through raids;
  - reports cover only battles the civilization fought.

  Every run must also replay exactly.

## W1b as built

- **Armoury.**
  - **Order.** `craft_equipment` names an item, a quantity of 1–100, and workers at one of the civilization's settlements. At least one worker must practise the item's capability. The materials leave the store at once.
  - **Work.** Each worker present adds a person-day each day, and finished items go into the store.
  - **Workers.** Armourers eat but neither farm nor do other work, and cannot be sent away until the job is done.
  - **Recipes:**

| Item | Materials | Person-days | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sling | 1 timber | 1 | — |
| Spear | 2 timber | 2 | timbercraft |
| Bow | 3 timber | 3 | archery |
| Bronze arms | 3 metal | 4 | bronze working |
| Ladders | 4 timber | 2 | — |
| Ram | 10 timber, 4 planks | 6 | timbercraft |
| Catapult | 15 timber, 10 stone | 10 | siegecraft |

  - **New capabilities.** Archery, bronze working and siegecraft now exist as capabilities, and are practised by whoever holds the skill. Discovering them by research comes with W1c; until then they spread only by teaching and migration.
- **Units.**
  - **Priority.** Kits go to fighters best first: bronze arms, axe, spear, bow, sling. Fighters left without one fight as levies.
  - **Melee strength:**
    - levy ×1.0;
    - slinger and archer ×0.9;
    - spearman ×1.4, and ×1.75 when defending;
    - axeman ×1.5, and ×1.65 when attacking;
    - heavy infantry ×1.8.
  - **Changes from the plan.**
    - Slingers and archers are weaker in melee than the plan's ×1.1 and ×1.2. Their strength is the opening volley, in which each slinger has a 5% and each archer a 10% chance of a hit.
    - Heavy infantry adds up to 5 points to its side's morale threshold, in proportion to its share of the side.
  - **Carried kits.** A war party carries one kit per fighter at most. Home defenders take kits from their store in the same order. The kits of fighters who die are lost.
- **Siege engines.**
  - **Weight.** Ladders take 4 carrying units, a ram 10 and a catapult 20. Rams and catapults are heavy and add half again to every tile the party crosses. The limit on unspent walking rose to fit the dearest slowed tile.
  - **Crews.** Engines need crews: 2 for ladders, 4 for a ram and 6 for a catapult, heaviest first. Crews are taken from the last fighters in id order and fight at half strength.
  - **Effects.** Engines matter only when assaulting an enemy settlement:
    - ladders halve the settlement's defence bonus;
    - a ram removes it;
    - each catapult has a 30% chance of a hit on the defenders before every round.
  - **Losing engines.** A routed party abandons its engines. Defenders at home take them into their store; on open ground they are lost.
- **Stress runs.** A new armoury matrix of 8 seeds has civilizations craft slings and ladders and raid with them. It checks every day that no kit or engine ever exists beyond what was made or held at the start, and that runs replay exactly.

## W1c as built

- **Research order.**
  - **Order.** `research` names a topic, scholars at one of the civilization's settlements, and a length of 1–180 days. It is refused for a topic that is already known, whose prerequisites are missing, or that is ordered twice in one council.
  - **Scholars.** Scholars eat but neither farm nor do other work, and cannot be sent away while the research runs.
- **Progress.** Each scholar present earns 1 point a day, and 6 if they practise writing. Points belong to the civilization and the topic, not to the assignment. Research that runs out of days before finishing is `research_paused`, and its points wait for the next assignment on that topic.
- **Discovery.** When a topic's points reach its cost:
  - every living scholar who worked on it becomes a practitioner, with skill 100;
  - the capability is recorded;
  - the points are spent;
  - any other assignments on that topic end.

  The knowledge then spreads by teaching and migration, and is forgotten when every practitioner dies, like any other.
- **New capabilities:** spear formations, drill doctrine, military logistics and fortification, alongside W1b's archery, bronze working and siegecraft. Costs and prerequisites are as in the table above.
- **Effects:**
  - **Spear formations:** spearmen of a civilization that knows it defend at ×1.375 instead of ×1.25, both at home and in war parties.
  - **Drill doctrine:** drill gives 2 points every 5 days, and its cap rises to 30.
  - **Military logistics:** fighters of a civilization that knows it carry 60 units each. The limit is fixed on the war party when it sets out.
  - **Fortification** is discovered now; walls use it in W2.
- **Stress runs.** In the armoury matrix, both sides study spear formations from the first council, and every run confirms both discover it.

## W2: occupation and sieges

W2 lets a winning side stay. A war party can camp outside a settlement and starve it, or beat its defenders and hold it. Walls and towers make storming costly, and raiders can wreck roads on enemy land. Occupation never changes a settlement's owner or its people's loyalty; surrender and allegiance are W3.

### Decisions

1. **Food:** every settlement keeps its own store, and goods are hauled between them.
2. **Occupiers** may requisition food from the occupied settlement's store.
3. **Walls:** five grades that are upgraded one step at a time, with towers on top.
4. **No surrender before W3.** A siege ends in starvation, a storm, a sally, relief, or the besiegers leaving.

W2 is built in three steps, each in its own pull request:
- **W2a:** settlement stores and hauling;
- **W2b:** walls and towers;
- **W2c:** sieges, occupation and wrecking roads.

## W2a: settlement stores and hauling

- **Stores.** `CivilizationState.inventory` stays as the capital's store, so a civilization with one settlement behaves exactly as before. Every other settlement gets a store of its own in `CivilizationState.stores`, keyed by settlement id and starting empty. Storage capacity is set per settlement.
- **Where people are fed.** People eat from the store of the settlement where they stand:
  - hungriest first within each settlement;
  - hunger, recovery and food reserve decrees are all worked out per settlement.
- **Where goods come from.** Every rule that takes goods takes them from the settlement where the work happens, and every rule that yields goods delivers them there:
  - farming, gathering and crafting;
  - construction, roadwork and the armoury;
  - research needs no goods.
- **Journeys** take goods and provisions from the settlement they leave from, and deliver leftovers and cargo to the settlement they reach. For people in the field, such as garrisons, the nearest settlement by travel cost stands in.
- **Tolls.** A toll post deposits into the store of the settlement at the end of its deposit route.
- **Storage capacity.** Each store holds a limited number of units, all goods counted together. Whatever does not fit is wasted, and farming stops at a full store. A settlement with no storehouse can keep 2 000 units.
- **Storehouses** stand in a settlement and are upgraded one grade at a time, like roads. Each adds its grade's room to its settlement's store:

| Grade | Adds | Step materials | Person-days | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Storage pit | 2 000 | — | 5 | — |
| Granary | 5 000 | 30 stone | 10 | — |
| Storehouse | 10 000 | 40 stone, 20 timber | 20 | timbercraft |
| Warehouse | 20 000 | 80 stone, 20 planks | 40 | stoneworking |
| Depot | 40 000 | 150 stone, 10 tools | 60 | stoneworking |

- **New order `build_storehouse`.**
  - **What it names:** builders standing together in one of the civilization's settlements, a target grade, and optionally a storehouse there to upgrade. Without one, a new storehouse is built from a storage pit upward.
  - **Materials:** every step's materials leave the settlement's store when the work begins.
  - **Craft:** each step that needs a craft needs a builder who practises it.
  - **One job at a time:** a storehouse can have only one job working on it.
  - **Room grows step by step.** Each finished grade adds its room at once, and `storehouse_built` or `storehouse_upgraded` fires.
  - **Builders** eat but neither farm nor travel. If every builder dies, the work stops, and the materials of the steps not yet built go back into the store.
- **The old `start_project` of kind `storage`** still builds one new granary, in the settlement where its builders stand.
- **Capital.** A capital starts with 5 granaries, giving 27 000 units, or more if its founders' goods need them.
- **Reports** show each store's capacity, the storehouses, and the work under way.
- **Later.** In W2c, raiders and occupiers may damage storehouses and knock them down a grade, as with walls.
- **New order `haul`.** A journey of the new kind `haul` carries goods between two of the civilization's own settlements. It follows the usual rules for route, provisions, capacity and hazards, and pays tolls on others' land.
- **Reports.** The council report shows every settlement's store, and the total.
- **Plunder** comes from the store of the settlement under attack, not the civilization's whole store.

## W2b: walls and towers

- **New order `build_walls`.**
  - **What it does:** builders standing together in one of the civilization's settlements raise its walls, one grade at a time, to a target grade.
  - **Materials:** every step's materials leave that settlement's store when the work begins.
  - **Craft:** each step needs a builder who practises the craft it needs.
  - **One job at a time:** a settlement's walls can have only one job at a time, and that includes tower work.
- **Grades:**

| Grade | Step materials | Person-days | Needs | Settlement defence | Strength | Towers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Earthwork | — | 30 | — | ×1.10 | 10 | 0 |
| Palisade | 40 timber | 60 | timbercraft | ×1.25 | 25 | 2 |
| Drystone wall | 80 stone | 120 | stoneworking | ×1.40 | 45 | 3 |
| Mortared wall | 150 stone, 10 tools | 200 | fortification | ×1.60 | 70 | 4 |
| Fortress wall | 250 stone, 20 tools | 300 | fortification | ×1.80 | 100 | 6 |

- **Progress.**
  - Each finished grade stands at once, at full strength, and keeps its towers. `walls_built` fires for the first grade and `walls_raised` for each one after.
  - Builders eat but neither farm nor travel.
  - If every builder dies, the materials for the steps not yet built go back into the store.
- **Defence.** In a battle at the settlement, the wall's bonus multiplies the settlement's own ×1.25 and the terrain's.
- **New order `build_towers`.**
  - **What it does:** it adds towers to standing walls, up to the grade's limit.
  - **Cost:** on a palisade, a tower costs 10 timber and 10 person-days and needs timbercraft. On stone walls it costs 20 stone and 20 person-days and needs stoneworking.
  - **Progress:** each tower stands as soon as its days are done, and `towers_built` fires.
- **Towers in battle.**
  - **Crew:** each tower needs two home defenders to man it, so five defenders man only two towers.
  - **Fire:** every manned tower has a 15% chance of a hit on the attackers in the opening volley and again before every round.
  - **The crews still fight at full strength.** At first they fought at half strength like engine crews, but then a small village lost more by manning its towers than its walls gave it.
- **Engines against walls:**
  - **Ladders** halve the bonus of earthwork, palisades and drystone walls, and do nothing against mortared or fortress walls.
  - **A ram** removes the bonus of the three lower grades, and halves it on mortared and fortress walls.
  - Both still cut the settlement's own ×1.25 as before.
- **Strength** is recorded and full when built.
- **Moved to W2c:** catapult damage, walls falling a grade, and repairs, because sieges are the first thing that damages walls.

## W2c: sieges

W2c is sieges alone. Occupation and wrecking roads move to W2d, to keep each change reviewable.

- **New war objective `besiege`.**
  - **Route:** it ends on a tile next to a settlement of the target that the sovereign knows of, and not on a settlement.
  - **Arrival:** the party camps there instead of turning home. A `Siege` is recorded in `WorldState.sieges`, `siege_began` fires, and the war starts at once if it had not.
  - **The camp** stays put, eating from its packs and foraging, and fights anyone of the enemy who comes to it.
- **Blockade.** While a camp stands:
  - no journey of the owner may set out from the besieged settlement, except a sally: a war party whose route ends on a besieging camp;
  - no journey of the owner may end at the besieged settlement;
  - residents may not farm, because the fields are outside;
  - the settlement is cut off from its civilization's supply, so its influence is halved, as for any cut-off settlement.
- **Bombardment.** Each day, every crewed catapult in the camp has a 30% chance of a hit.
  - **Against walls:** a hit knocks 5 points off their strength. At zero the walls fall one grade (`walls_fell`); the grade below stands at full strength, and towers beyond its limit fall with it. Earthwork that falls leaves no walls.
  - **With no walls:** a hit wounds an able person in the settlement.
- **How a siege ends** (`siege_lifted`, with the reason):
  - **starved:** the camp's packs hold less than the walk home needs, so it marches home;
  - **too few:** fewer than 4 besiegers are left alive;
  - **recalled:** the new order `lift_siege`;
  - **broken:** relief or a sally beats the camp, which is routed home;
  - **stormed:** the new order `storm` sends the camp into the settlement. It fights a normal battle there, with walls, towers and engines, then raids or turns home like any war party.
- **Starvation.** With no farming and nothing coming in, residents eat through their own store and then go hungry, as hunger already works.
- **New order `repair_walls`.** Builders at the settlement restore damaged walls to full strength. It costs a quarter of the grade's materials, rounded up, and a quarter of its person-days. It can be done during a siege.
- **Reports.** Each side sees its sieges: the besieged at once, the besiegers from their own camp.
- **Moved to W3:** surrender on terms.

### W2c as built

- **Storming.** A storm adds the settlement tile to the camp's route and ends the siege as `stormed`. The party walks in the next day and fights there with every wall, tower and engine effect, then raids or turns home as its storm order said.
- **Relief and sallies** need no new mechanism. A war party of the owner whose route ends on the camp fights it as it would any enemy party. A routed camp breaks, and its siege ends as `broken`.
- **One battle a day.** A war party now fights at most one battle a day. Before this, a camp routed by relief turned round on the same tile and fought again the same day. That bug had existed since W1a.
- **Starvation check.** A camp goes home when its packs hold less than its living fighters need for the walk home, counted on the camp's route without the slowing of heavy engines.
- **Limitation:** foreign journeys, such as a partner's shipment, are not stopped by a blockade. Only the besieged civilization's own orders are.
- **Stress runs.** A new siege matrix of 8 seeds runs camps with a catapult against earthwork, palisades and drystone walls. The defenders mend damaged walls, and the attackers storm or lift. Every day it checks that the world is valid, that camps never move, and that nothing leaves a besieged settlement. Every run replays exactly.

## W2d: occupation and wrecking roads

### Occupation

- **New objective `occupy`.** A war party that wins a battle at an enemy settlement stays there as occupiers.
- **Records.** The owner keeps the settlement and its claim. Occupations are recorded in a new `occupations` list in the world state.
- **Territory.**
  - The occupied settlement stops anchoring its owner's influence.
  - The occupiers project a garrison's influence of their own.
  - Tiles that depended on the settlement are cut off from their owner's supply.
- **Residents** stay loyal and keep living and farming there. Their sovereign cannot send them on journeys.
- **Requisition.** Occupiers take their daily food from the settlement's store before the residents eat. They also take any toll chest on the tile.
- **Storehouses.** Occupiers may burn a storehouse, knocking it down a grade.
- **How occupation ends:**
  - the occupiers leave, or starve;
  - a force of the owner beats them;
  - the residents rise, when their able people outnumber the occupiers three to one.

### Wrecking roads

- **New war party flag `wreck_roads`.** On enemy land, the party stops a day on each road tile and pulls it down one grade.

### Events

`settlement_occupied`, `occupation_ended`, `residents_rose`, `storehouse_burned`, `road_wrecked`.

### W2d as built

- **Occupying.** A war party with the `occupy` objective marches on a settlement of the target that it knows. If it wins the battle there, or finds no one to fight, it stays and holds the settlement. The party holds its tile the way a siege camp does. An `Occupation` is recorded in `WorldState.occupations`, `settlement_occupied` fires, and both sides see it in their reports. The owner keeps the settlement, its claim and the tile.
- **Living among the residents.** Occupiers do not fight the residents each day. They fight only an enemy war party that comes to them, and a rising.
- **Rising.** When the residents' able people number at least three times the occupiers, `residents_rose` fires and they fight. If the occupiers lose, they are routed home and the occupation ends as `rose`.
- **Requisition.** Each day the occupiers take their day's food from the settlement's store into their packs, before the residents eat. They also empty any toll chest on the tile into their plunder, up to what they can carry.
- **Territory.** An occupied settlement no longer anchors its owner's influence. The occupiers project a garrison's influence from it, so the land around it drifts to them.
- **Residents** cannot be sent on any journey, and none of their civilization's journeys may end there (`occupied`).
- **New order `burn_storehouse`.** Occupiers name a storehouse in the settlement they hold, and it falls one grade; a burned storage pit is gone. The store loses that grade's room at once. Goods that no longer fit are lost, food first (`storehouse_burned`).
- **Withdrawing.** `lift_siege` also recalls occupiers, who march home with what they took. Occupiers also leave when fewer than 4 are left, or when the store is empty and their packs will not last the walk home. A relieving force that beats them ends the occupation as `beaten`.
- **Wrecking roads.** A war party sent with `wreck_roads` stops on each road tile of land held by its target, or by anyone it is at war with. It spends a day there pulling the road down one grade, and a wrecked footpath is gone (`road_wrecked`). Each tile is wrecked once per party.
- **Stress runs.** A new occupation matrix of 8 seeds runs road-wrecking war parties that occupy a settlement, burn a storehouse a council later, and then withdraw. Every day it checks that the world is valid, that occupiers never move, that nobody leaves an occupied settlement, and that no store holds more than its capacity. Every run replays exactly.

## W3: prisoners, peace and allegiance

W3 lets a war end: prisoners taken in battle, peace treaties with a truce and terms, and settlements that change hands with their people.

### Decisions

1. **Prisoners** are taken in the rout and made to work for their captors. Some escape each month.
2. **Surrender terms:**
   - tribute in goods;
   - a ceded settlement;
   - whether the prisoners are kept or freed.
3. **Allegiance.** People who change civilization keep everything personal: their id, family, health, life history and knowledge. A quarter of each skill is held back for a year while they settle in.
4. **Peace.** The existing `peace` treaty ends a war and starts a truce of a set number of days. Breaking the truce is a treaty breach.
5. **Later.** The spec's "release people to join another civilization" goes to W4, together with dissolution.

W3 is built in three steps, each in its own pull request:
- **W3a:** prisoners;
- **W3b:** peace, truce, tribute and prisoner terms;
- **W3c:** ceding settlements and allegiance transfer.

## W3a: prisoners

- **Capture.** When a side breaks and the winners hold the field, part of the rout becomes capture instead of wounds.
  - **Who:** each pursuit hit on a fleeing fighter captures them instead, with a 40% chance.
  - **Limit:** captives cannot outnumber twice the winners standing.
  - **Condition:** captives keep whatever wounds they already had.
- **Where captives are held.**
  - **Home defenders** keep their captives in the settlement.
  - **A war party** takes its captives along. They walk with the party and eat from its packs, and they are held at the party's home settlement when it returns.
  - **If the party is routed or wiped out,** its captives are freed where they stand.
- **Captives stay citizens of their own civilization.** They are marked with their captor and the settlement holding them. They cannot be ordered by their own sovereign, cannot fight for either side, and are not counted as defenders.
- **Labour and food.**
  - Captives eat from the store of the settlement holding them.
  - They farm there alongside its people, adding to what the fields yield up to the land's limit.
  - They cannot be given other work, drill, research or journeys.
- **Escape.** At every council, each captive has a 10% chance to escape. Captives walk home on their own on the shortest known way.
- **Order `release_prisoners`.** The captor names captives who walk home the same way. Peace terms (W3b) can also free them.
- **Reports.** Each side sees its captives and its own people held captive, by name.
- **Events:** `captured`, `captive_escaped`, `captives_released`, `captive_home`.

### W3a as built

- **Capture** happens in the pursuit. Each blow on a fleeing fighter has a 40% chance to take them alive instead, until the winners hold twice their standing number.
  - **How often:** the pursuit strikes only a tenth of the broken side, so about 4% of a routed force is captured. Prisoners are a real but modest outcome of a rout.
  - **Tests** raise the chance to 100% where they need a certain capture.
- **Who holds the captives.**
  - **Home defenders** hold them in the settlement where they fought.
  - **A winning war party** takes them along in `captive_ids`. They walk with it, eat from its packs, and are held at its home settlement when it returns.
  - **A battle on open ground** with no war party to keep the captives lets them go.
  - **Fighters taken from a party** leave it; a party taken whole is ended.
- **Captive records.** Captives stay citizens of their own civilization, marked with `captive_of` and `held_at`.
  - Their own sovereign cannot name them in any order (`held_captive`), and council reports leave them out of `person_ids`.
  - They are never defenders or enemies on a tile, and are not counted as residents.
- **Food and work.**
  - A captive eats from the store of the settlement holding them, and their own civilization stops feeding them.
  - Captives farm there, adding to what the fields yield.
  - Occupiers also requisition food for their captives.
- **Freeing captives.** Captives are freed by escape (10% each at every council), by `release_prisoners`, or when their captors are routed or perish. `captive_freed` fires with the reason: `escaped`, `released` or `rescued`. The freed walk home to their nearest settlement by the shortest way over land, as a relocation of up to 16.
- **Reports:** `captives`, the prisoners a civilization holds, and `held_captive`, its own people held by others.
- **Checks.** The world validator checks every day that each living captive is held at one of the captor's settlements, or marches with one of its war parties.
- **Stress runs.** A new prisoner matrix of 8 seeds has both sides raid each other at every council, with capture certain. One side lets half its captives go. Every day it checks that captives never fight or travel free, and every run replays exactly.

## W3b: peace, truce and tribute

- **Peace treaty terms.** A `peace` treaty offer, sent and accepted by envoys like any treaty, carries:
  - **truce days:** 30–365;
  - **prisoners:** whether each side frees the captives it holds or keeps them;
  - **tribute:** goods one side pays the other, once or every month for a number of months.
- **When it takes effect.**
  - The war ends.
  - Sieges are lifted and occupations end with the reason `peace`, and their parties march home.
  - Freed prisoners walk home.
- **Truce.** Until the truce ends, neither side may send a war party against the other, lay a siege or occupy. Such orders are refused, and an attack made anyway breaks the treaty as a breach.
- **Tribute moves physically.** The payer sends each payment with a `dispatch_shipment` under the peace treaty.
  - The treaty records what is owed and when, and what arrived.
  - A payment not delivered 30 days after it fell due is a breach.
  - A breach ends the treaty, and a new war can start.
- **Surrender** is a peace treaty offered on the loser's terms, with the tribute running to the winner.

### W3b as built

- **Terms.** `PeaceTerms` rides on a peace offer (`offer_treaty` with `peace_terms`). It carries the truce days (30–365), whether each side frees the other's captives, and optional tribute: goods per payment, the payer, and 1–24 payments. The terms are copied onto the treaty when the acceptance reaches the proposer.
- **When peace takes effect** (`peace_made`):
  - the war between the two ends;
  - sieges are lifted and occupations end, both with the reason `peace`, and their parties march home;
  - war parties still heading out against each other turn back;
  - captives are freed as the terms say, and walk home.
- **Truce.** While it lasts, a war party against the other side is refused (`truce`). A declaration of war still breaks the peace, as the declarer's breach, and war parties may then march.
- **Tribute.**
  - **Due dates:** payment *k* falls due 30 × *k* days after the peace; the first a month after, so a ruler has a council in which to send it. Each payment has 30 days of grace.
  - **Shipping:** the payer ships tribute with `dispatch_shipment` under the peace treaty, which only the payer may do. What arrives is added to the treaty's `tribute_received` (`tribute_received` event).
  - **Default:** tribute still missing after its grace breaks the treaty as the payer's breach (`tribute_defaulted`).
- **Reports** now list the treaties a civilization is party to, so a ruler can see its peace, its truce and what it owes.
- **Stress runs.** A new peace matrix of 6 seeds sends real envoys with an offer and an acceptance after a raid, then ships tribute each month. Every day it checks that no war party sets out during the truce, and every run replays exactly. In one seed a payment arrives too late and the payer defaults.

## W3c: ceding settlements and allegiance

- **Ceding.** A peace treaty may cede one of the payer's settlements to the other side; never a capital. When the treaty takes effect:
  - the settlement, its store, storehouses, walls and toll posts change hands;
  - everyone living there changes allegiance: people at the settlement who are not away on a journey or held captive elsewhere.
- **Allegiance transfer.**
  - A person moves to the new civilization with their id, family ties, health, pregnancy and life history.
  - A quarter of each skill is held back for 365 days and then restored.
  - Capabilities they practise go with them, so knowledge spreads as it does through migration.
  - The move is recorded as an allegiance change, with the day and the reason.
- **Territory** follows at once: the settlement anchors its new owner's influence.
- **Events:** `settlement_ceded`, `allegiance_changed`.

### W3c as built

- **Changing allegiance** is one engine step, used by both migration and cession.
  - **What moves:** a person moves to the new civilization with their id, family ties, health and any pregnancy.
  - **Record:** an `AllegianceChange` records the day, both civilizations and the reason, `migration` or `cession`.
  - **Skills:** a quarter of each skill, rounded down, is held back in `held_skills` and restored on `settled_day`, 365 days later. Anyone who practised a skill still practises it.
  - **Knowledge:** capabilities practised by the newcomers become known to their new civilization, as migration already worked.
- **Leaving old duties.** People who leave drop out of their old civilization's drills, workshops, research, storehouse and wall work, teaching and garrisons. Work left with no one is dropped.
- **Ceding.**
  - **The term:** `PeaceTerms.ceded_settlement` names a settlement of either side except its capital. It is checked when the offer is sent, and again when the peace takes effect.
  - **Everything goes with it:** the settlement changes hands with its store, storehouses and walls, and with a toll post at its gate.
  - **Lost with the settlement:** the old owner's work there is dropped. Its toll posts that emptied their chests there lapse, and their chests go into the ceded store.
  - **Captives:** those the old owner held there are let go.
  - **People:** everyone at the settlement who is not away or held captive changes allegiance (`settlement_ceded`).
- **Territory** follows by itself: the settlement anchors its new owner's influence from the next day.
- **Settlement ids** are never reused. A civilization's next settlement is numbered one past the highest it ever founded, wherever those settlements now belong.
- **Stress runs.** A new cession matrix of 6 seeds runs a raid, then a peace offer that cedes a colony, carried and accepted by real envoys. Every day it checks that each person belongs to exactly one civilization, and that anyone who changed allegiance is recorded as belonging where they are. Every run replays exactly.

## W4: release, dissolution, elimination and endings

W4 completes the lifecycle of a civilization. A ruler may release people to join another civilization. A civilization with no working settlement breaks up over time. One with no living members is eliminated and leaves ruins. The world records exactly when a single civilization, or none, remains.

### Decisions

1. **Breakup.** A civilization with no working settlement has a grace period. If it has not recovered after that, its people drift away each month to the nearest civilization they know.
2. **Release.** Released people walk to the other civilization, whose council may admit or refuse them. Refused people walk home.
3. **Ruins.** An eliminated civilization's settlements become ruins that anyone can salvage or resettle.
4. **Endings.** When one civilization, or none, remains, the ending is recorded exactly and the world keeps running.

W4 is built in two steps, each in its own pull request:
- **W4a:** release and petitions;
- **W4b:** dissolution, elimination, ruins and endings.

## W4a: release and petitions

- **New order `release_people`.**
  - **Who:** people standing together at one of the civilization's settlements, free and able to travel, released to a civilization it knows.
  - **How they go:** they walk the given route to that civilization's known settlement as petitioners, a journey of the new kind `petition`, with provisions from their settlement's store.
- **Waiting.** On arrival they wait outside the settlement, eating from their packs or foraging. The receiving council sees them in its report as `petitioners`.
- **New order `answer_petition`.**
  - **Admit:** the petitioners change allegiance on the spot (`release`), with the usual year of held-back skill.
  - **Refuse:** they walk home.
  - **No answer:** petitioners still unanswered at the council after the one they arrived for are refused.
- **Events:** `people_released`, `petition_arrived`, `petition_admitted`, `petition_refused`.

## W4b: dissolution, elimination, ruins and endings

- **Working settlement.** A settlement works while at least one of its own free people lives there and no enemy occupies it.
- **Homeless.** A civilization with no working settlement is `homeless` from that day (`civilization_homeless`), and recovers the day one works again (`civilization_recovered`).
- **Breakup.** After 90 days homeless, at every council, a quarter of its free people, at least one, set out as petitioners (W4a) to the nearest civilization they know. Those refused come back and try again at a later council.
- **Elimination.** A civilization with no living members is eliminated (`civilization_eliminated`):
  - its wars end;
  - its treaties end;
  - the captives it held are freed;
  - its settlements become ruins, keeping their stores, storehouses and walls.
- **Ruins.**
  - **New order `salvage`:** carriers walk from one of their settlements to a ruin, load what they can bear from its store, and carry it home.
  - **Resettling:** a founding party that settles on a ruin takes over its store, storehouses and walls.
- **Endings.** The world records each ending once, the day it happens, in `WorldState.endings`:
  - the day only one civilization with living members remains (`last_civilization`), with its name and population;
  - the day none remains (`no_civilization`).

  Nothing stops, and the world keeps running.
