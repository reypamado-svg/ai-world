# War, Occupation and Surrender Plan

**Goal:** Civilizations can fight. War is carried out by real people who march, eat, fight and die. Its consequences are permanent: deaths, wounds, prisoners, lost goods, occupied land and, in the end, surrender and changes of allegiance. This is slice 5 of `2026-09-29-territory.md`. It covers the conflict and endgame parts of the civilization-layer design (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-27-civilization-layer-design.md`).

This is research and design only; nothing here is built yet.

## What the design spec requires

- **Combat factors.** Combat outcomes depend on numbers, training, equipment, terrain, fortification, preparation, leadership, morale, weather, intelligence and supply.
- **Consequences.** Conflict produces permanent deaths, injuries, prisoners, displacement, destroyed assets, resource loss, disease risk, and delayed or incorrect reports.
- **Campaigns** are multi-day projects with explicit people, equipment, supply requirements, objectives and routes. A command never creates a combat outcome by itself.
- **Loyalty and surrender.**
  - Citizens stay loyal while their civilization functions, and occupation alone never transfers allegiance.
  - A sovereign may surrender, release its people, and direct them to join another civilization.
  - A society with no functioning settlements may dissolve gradually.
- **Endings.** A civilization is eliminated only when no living member remains. One-survivor and zero-survivor endings are recorded exactly.
- **No forced conflict.** The engine never forces a final war; a long peace is a valid outcome.

## What exists today

| Building block | State | Use in war |
|---|---|---|
| People | Sex, age, health (basis points), hunger debt, disease load, skills, location, and permanent death | Fighters, casualties, wounds |
| Journeys | Explicit routes, terrain costs, roads, provisions, foraging, hazards, and turning back | Campaigns are journeys that fight |
| Garrisons and settlements | Defenders standing on a tile | Targets and defenders |
| Territory | Derived control with challenges, supply cut-off already defined (halved influence), and `route_severed` events | Occupation and cut supply lines |
| Goods | Stores, toll chests, cargo in transit, and axes, tools and metal in stores | Plunder and equipment |
| Treaties | Trade and migration terms, cancellation and repudiation; the `peace` kind exists but does nothing | War breaks treaties, and peace ends war |
| Missing | Weather, fortifications, a fighting skill, prisoners, and a record of allegiance history | Added below or deferred |

## Research summary

- **Pre-state and early warfare was mostly raids.**
  - Keeley (*War Before Civilization*, 1996) and Gat (*War in Human Civilization*, 2006) find that ambushes and raids, not pitched battles, did most of the killing among small societies.
  - Our civilizations start with 32 founders, so the first wars will look like raids between villages, not clashes of armies.
- **Numbers help, though not as the square.** Lanchester (1916) showed that aimed fire makes strength grow with the square of numbers. Hand-to-hand fighting is closer to his *linear* law, where each fighter engages only a few enemies at a time. Numbers matter, but not quadratically.
- **Most deaths come in the rout.**
  - Krentz ("Casualties in Hoplite Battles", 1985) estimates victors lost about 5% and the defeated about 14%.
  - Ancient accounts agree that most killing happened after a line broke and fled.
  - Battles were won by **breaking morale**, not by killing everyone.
- **The defender's edge.** Rough terrain, walls and preparation favour defenders. The "3:1" attacker rule of thumb is disputed, but the direction is not.
- **Supply limits campaigns.**
  - Engels (*Alexander the Great and the Logistics of the Macedonian Army*, 1978) puts a soldier's need at about 1.4 kg of grain a day.
  - An army could carry only a couple of weeks' food, and foraging stripped the land it passed through.
  - Our provisions and foraging model already captures this.
- **Sieges.** Most pre-modern sieges ended through hunger, disease, betrayal or negotiated surrender rather than storming, and surrender "on terms" was the norm.
- **Occupation is not ownership.** Under the Hague Regulations (1907, Article 43), an occupier administers territory but does not acquire sovereignty. *Europa Universalis IV* likewise keeps the owner and the occupying controller separate. Our territory layer already separates claims from control, and occupation becomes a third layer.
- **Game precedents.**
  - *Crusader Kings III:* levies, supply limits, and attrition in hostile land.
  - *Dwarf Fortress:* individual wounds and deaths.
  - *Battle Brothers* and *Total War:* morale breaks decide fights.
  - *Europa Universalis IV:* occupation and negotiated peace.
  - The shared lesson: resolve fights through **morale, terrain and supply**, and record every casualty by name.

## Proposed slicing

War is too large for one change. Recommended order:

1. **W1: hostility, war parties and combat.** Declaring war, raiding and attacking, battles that kill, wound and rout real people, and plunder.
2. **W2: occupation and sieges.** Holding enemy land and settlements, cutting supply, sieges that starve rather than storm, and destroying roads and seizing chests.
3. **W3: prisoners, surrender and allegiance.** Captives, surrender terms, allegiance transfer that keeps personal history, and peace treaties that end wars.
4. **W4: dissolution, elimination and the end of the world.** A civilization with no functioning settlements dissolves, one with no living members is eliminated, and one-survivor and zero-survivor endings are detected.

## W1 in detail

### 1. Hostility

- **New order `declare_war`.** It is sent to a known civilization as a diplomatic message carried by an envoy, like a treaty cancellation.
- **Breaking treaties.** On dispatch, the declarer is at war and every treaty between the two is repudiated. That treaty breach already has rules and events. A civilization cannot declare war on another and stay bound to it by treaty.
- **When the target learns.** The target learns it is at war when the declaration is delivered, or earlier if it is attacked. A surprise attack before delivery is possible and recorded as such.
- **War state.** Each war is recorded with the day it started, the declarer, and each side's first knowledge of it. War between the two continues until a peace treaty (W3) or until one side is eliminated (W4).
- **Undeclared raids are not allowed.** Every attack needs a war on record. This keeps treaties meaningful, and it is simple to relax later if you want raids without declaration.

### 2. War parties

- **New order `send_war_party`.** It is a journey of a new kind, `campaign`. Up to 16 fighters from one of the civilization's settlements march an explicit route over known land, with provisions and optional equipment taken from the store.
- **Equipment.** Axes, and later metal weapons, are carried one per fighter and return home with survivors.
- **Objectives**, set at the end of the route:
  - **raid:** attack whoever defends the target tile. If the fight is won, carry off goods from the store or chest there, up to what the party can bear, and march home. Plunder is taken only when fighters physically stand in the settlement.
  - **attack:** fight the defenders of a garrison or settlement, then hold the tile. Holding it becomes occupation in W2; in W1 the party then marches home.
- **Encounters on the road.** A war party that enters a tile holding hostile people fights them. This covers garrisons, residents, and other war parties. Hostile shipments and migrants on the road are ambushed: their goods are seized and they flee home. Capturing them waits for W3.
- **Everything else is as for other journeys.** War parties travel on terrain and roads, pay no tolls to enemies, and suffer delays, hazards and hunger.

### 3. Combat

**Fighting strength**, in integer basis points, for each fighter is 100 multiplied by these factors, all rounded deterministically:

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Health | health_bp / 10 000 |
| Age | full from 16 to 50 years; 60% from 13 to 15 and from 51 to 60; otherwise not a fighter |
| Hunger | −1% for each point of nutrition debt, down to −50% |
| Arms | +50% with an axe, +80% with a metal weapon (later) |
| Experience | +1% for each point of a new `arms` skill, which is gained by surviving battles, up to +50% |

**Defender bonuses:**

- ×1.25 on forest;
- ×1.5 on mountain;
- ×1.25 inside a settlement.

Fortifications arrive with a later building slice; the multiplier is defined now so they can plug in.

**A battle is fought in daily rounds** on one tile.

- **Losses.** Each round, each side suffers losses that grow with the other side's strength relative to its own. The rate is calibrated so that an even fight costs each side about 3–5% a day.
- **Wounds and deaths.**
  - A loss is a **wound**: health falls by 3 000–6 000.
  - A wounded fighter whose health would drop to 0 dies. Deaths are permanent.
  - Every person-level outcome is drawn from a named stream, `day:{d}:war:{battle}:…`, so replays match exactly.
- **Morale and rout.** A side **breaks** when its losses in the battle pass its morale threshold:
  - 30% for a war party;
  - 50% for defenders of their own settlement.

  Hunger and being cut off from supply lower the threshold by up to half.
- **After a break:**
  - The routed side flees. Attackers flee home; defenders flee to their nearest own settlement, or scatter and hide if none is reachable.
  - Pursuit costs the routed side a further 10% of its strength that day, which reproduces the historical pattern that most deaths come in the rout.
- **The winner** keeps the tile and gains `arms` experience.
- **Non-combatants.**
  - Children, the elderly and the sick in a settlement never fight.
  - A settlement whose defenders break is **taken**. Raiders plunder it. Occupation, and what happens to its people, come in W2 and W3.

### 4. Knowledge and reports

- **Each side learns only what its own people witnessed.** That covers the enemy it saw, its own dead and wounded by name, and the other side's losses as an estimate rounded to the nearest 5 people. The estimate is exact only for enemy dead left on the field.
- **Reports from a war party reach home only when survivors return.** A party that dies to the last person is known only as missing. This is the "delayed or incorrect reports" rule.
- **Reports gain:**
  - `wars`;
  - `war_reports`, per battle as known;
  - known enemy parties, as sighted.

### 5. Events

- `war_declared`, `war_learned`;
- `war_party_dispatched`, `battle_joined`, `battle_round`, `side_broke`, `battle_won`;
- `person_wounded`, and `person_died` with the cause `battle`;
- `settlement_raided`, `goods_plundered`, `convoy_ambushed`;
- `war_party_returned`.

## Open questions for the user

1. **How war starts.** Options:
   - **Recommended:** a formal declaration only, carried by an envoy, which breaks all treaties at once. Surprise attacks are possible before the declaration arrives.
   - Also allow undeclared raids with no declaration at all.
2. **Size of war parties.** Options:
   - **Recommended:** 16, like other journeys. With 32 founders that is half a civilization.
   - A larger limit for later, bigger populations.
3. **Fighting skill.** Options:
   - **Recommended:** `arms` experience from battles only.
   - Also a peacetime drilling order that trains it at the cost of work days.
4. **Slice order.** Options:
   - **Recommended:** W1 first, as above.
   - Another order.

## Validation (W1, when built)

- **Focused tests:**
  - war needs a declaration on record, and declaring breaks every treaty;
  - the target learns of the war on delivery or on attack;
  - fighting strength follows health, age, hunger, arms and experience;
  - terrain and settlement bonuses help defenders;
  - daily losses, wounds and deaths are permanent and named;
  - a side breaks at its morale threshold, and the rout costs more;
  - raiders plunder only on a win, and only what they can carry;
  - ambushed convoys lose their goods;
  - each side's report holds only what it witnessed, and arrives late or never.
- **Acceptance test:** two neighbours go from trade to war. A raid is repulsed, a second raid succeeds and carries off food, casualties are recorded by name on both sides, and replays and hashes match.
- **Stress tests:** war matrices across seeds check every day that:
  - no one fights who is not at war;
  - the dead never fight or return;
  - goods are conserved through plunder;
  - no report holds knowledge its civilization never witnessed.
